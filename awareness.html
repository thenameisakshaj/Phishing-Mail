Defensive learning centre

Recognise the message behind the manipulation.

Phishing is fraudulent communication designed to make a person reveal information, transfer money, open harmful content, or weaken security. It often combines a believable story with pressure and a risky action.

Message anatomy

How a phishing email is structured

01

Claimed identity

A familiar authority, colleague, or service is imitated.

02

Emotional trigger

Fear, curiosity, scarcity, urgency, or reward narrows attention.

03

Requested action

The reader is asked to click, reply, pay, share, or open a file.

04

Harmful outcome

Information, money, access, or device security may be compromised.

!

Fear and urgency

Threats of suspension, penalties, or immediate loss discourage careful checking.

♜

Authority impersonation

A message may claim to come from leadership, support, a bank, or an administrator.

⌛

Scarcity

Limited time or availability creates pressure to act before verifying.

?

Curiosity

Unexpected documents, private news, or vague alerts tempt the reader to click.

◇

Financial reward

Prizes, refunds, commissions, or impossible returns can hide payment fraud.

⌁

Account warnings

Security alerts may push a fake verification process or rushed credential entry.

•••

Credential theft

Passwords, OTPs, PINs, and recovery codes should never be shared through email.

▱

Malicious attachments

Unexpected archives, executables, scripts, or macro documents can carry harmful code.

↗

Link manipulation

Displayed text, confusing subdomains, redirects, and look-alike characters can conceal destinations.

Prevention checklist

How to Verify a Suspicious Email

  • Check the complete sender address.
  • Hover over links before opening them.
  • Do not share passwords or OTPs.
  • Contact the organisation using its official website or phone number.
  • Avoid opening unexpected attachments.
  • Check spelling and domain names.
  • Use multi-factor authentication.
  • Report suspicious emails.
  • Update security software.
  • Verify payment requests through another communication channel.
Defensive response

What to Do After Clicking a Phishing Link

  1. Disconnect from the network if malware is suspected.
  2. Change affected passwords from a trusted device.
  3. Enable multi-factor authentication.
  4. Contact the relevant service provider.
  5. Notify the bank if payment information was entered.
  6. Run a trusted security scan.
  7. Report the incident.
  8. Monitor account activity.
